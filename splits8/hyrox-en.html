HYROX,
start to finish.

What is HYROX

8 runs,
8 stations.

HYROX is a hybrid fitness race: 1 km of running, then one workout station, repeated 8 times. The order and distances are the same everywhere in the world, so a time run in Seoul compares directly with a time run in London.

There are no qualifiers and no time limit, so anyone who signs up can race. Mixed age and gender heats start at set intervals all day, and according to the official site more than 98% of athletes finish. Besides the solo race, there are Doubles (two people share the work) and Relay (four people share the work).

8 km

8 × 1 km of running. Running is close to half of the race time.

8 stations

Ski, sled push and pull, burpees, row, farmers carry, lunges, wall balls.

Roxzone

The transition area between the running lap and the stations. This time counts toward your result.

About 1h 30m

Average finish time for men in Open. For women it is about 1h 36m.

History

From Hamburg
to the world.

  1. 2017Started in Hamburg, Germany. Founded by Christian Toetzke, who ran large marathon events, and Moritz Fürste, an Olympic hockey gold medalist. The idea was "a marathon for people who train in the gym."
  2. 2018First race in Hamburg, with about 650 athletes. The first men's world record, 59:07 (Lukas Storath), was set the same year.
  3. 2019First World Championships (Oberhausen, Germany). A World Championships has been held every season since.
  4. 2020Because of COVID, the World Championships were held indoors on treadmills (AirRunner).
  5. 2022Sled weights changed to "including the sled," making each sled about 10 kg lighter. Many records fell that season.
  6. 202365 races in one year, about 175,000 athletes. 24,000 at the London race alone.
  7. 2024National team relay introduced. Partner gyms passed 5,000 worldwide.
  8. 2026World Championships in Stockholm, Sweden. In April in Warsaw, both the men's and women's solo world records were broken.

Get faster

Where does
the time go?

Race data shows that the gap between regular athletes and the elite is concentrated in a few places.

1 · Running on tired legs

Running is half the race.

The biggest part of the gap between the elite and regular athletes (around 90 minutes) is running. The average regular male athlete runs the first 1 km in 4:39 but slows to 6:10 on the last 1 km. Practice running on heavy legs right after a station (station → straight into 1 km).

2 · The first 1 km

Hold back at the start.

One analysis shows that regular male athletes are only 20 seconds behind the elite on the first 1 km, but close to 2 minutes behind by the end. Starting the first run a little slower than your target pace ends up faster.

3 · Sled Pull

If the pull takes twice as long as the push.

Elite athletes take about 20–30% longer on the sled pull than on the push, while regular athletes take 70–80% longer. Pull the rope hand over hand in short, quick pulls, and lean your whole body back to use your body weight. Pull practice gives the biggest return.

4 · Roxzone

2 free minutes.

In the transition area, elite athletes spend about 28 seconds each time, while athletes finishing in 90+ minutes spend about 52 seconds. When a station ends, don't stand still to catch your breath; start running right away, even slowly. If transitions take more than 6.5% of your total time, there is a lot to gain.

5 · Wall Balls

The longest station.

On average this station takes the longest (median 6:44). Instead of trying to go unbroken and falling apart, split the reps into sets you decide in advance (e.g. 15·15·15…) and keep the rests short.

6 · Burpees · Lunges

Keep your rhythm.

Burpee broad jumps and sandbag lunges are where the gap to the elite grows large. Moving at a steady rhythm without stopping is faster than trying to jump far.
